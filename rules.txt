TIMETABLE GENERATION RULES

====================================================
HARD CONSTRAINTS (MUST ALWAYS BE SATISFIED)
====================================================

1. COMPLETE WEEK

Each section has:
- 6 working days
- 9 periods per day

Total:
54 periods per week

Every section must have exactly 54 scheduled periods.

====================================================

2. WEEKLY SUBJECT COUNTS MUST MATCH EXACTLY

Each class has predefined weekly subject requirements.

Example:

Class VI

FL = 12
SL = 8
ENG = 12
MATH = 14
PET = 2
HW = 6

The timetable must contain exactly these counts.

No more.
No less.

====================================================

3. TEACHER ALLOCATIONS MUST MATCH EXACTLY

Teacher allocations are the source of truth.

Example:

Teacher: Archana
Section: VIA
Subject: FL
Weekly Periods: 12

The timetable must schedule exactly 12 FL periods for Archana in VIA.

No more.
No less.

====================================================

4. ONE SUBJECT PER SECTION PER PERIOD

A section can have only one subject in a period.

Example:

Monday Period 1

Allowed:
Maths

Not Allowed:
Maths + English

====================================================

5. ONE TEACHER PER PERIOD

A teacher cannot teach more than one section at the same time.

Example:

Monday Period 3

Not Allowed:
Archana teaching VIA and VIB simultaneously.

====================================================

6. NO EMPTY PERIODS

Every timetable slot must contain a subject.

All 54 slots must be filled.

====================================================

7. DAILY SUBJECT LIMIT

A subject cannot exceed:

ceil(weekly_periods / 6)

Example:

Maths = 14 periods/week

Maximum Maths periods per day:

ceil(14 / 6) = 3

Therefore Maths cannot appear more than 3 times on a single day.

====================================================

8. CONSECUTIVE SUBJECT LIMIT

A subject cannot be scheduled for more than 2 consecutive periods on the same day.

Allowed:

Maths
Maths

Not Allowed:

Maths
Maths
Maths

====================================================

9. VALID TEACHER-SUBJECT ASSIGNMENT

A teacher may only teach sections and subjects explicitly assigned in Teacher Allocations.

Example:

If Archana is assigned:

VIA FL = 12

Then Archana cannot be scheduled for:

VIA Maths
VIB English
VIIA PET

unless those allocations exist.

====================================================

10. WEEKLY TEACHER WORKLOAD LIMIT

A teacher cannot exceed their assigned weekly workload.

The total scheduled periods for a teacher must exactly match the sum of all their allocations.

====================================================


====================================================
SOFT CONSTRAINTS (OPTIMIZE WHEN POSSIBLE)
====================================================

11. EVEN SUBJECT DISTRIBUTION

Distribute subjects as evenly as possible across all 6 days.

Avoid placing most periods of a subject on only one or two days.

====================================================

12. HANDWRITING DISTRIBUTION

Spread Handwriting periods across multiple days.

Avoid clustering them together.

====================================================

13. PET DISTRIBUTION

Spread PET periods across the week.

Avoid scheduling both PET periods on the same day.

====================================================

14. SUBJECT POSITION VARIETY

Avoid placing the same subject in the same period slot every day.

Bad Example:

Mon P1 Maths
Tue P1 Maths
Wed P1 Maths
Thu P1 Maths
Fri P1 Maths
Sat P1 Maths

====================================================

15. BALANCED TEACHER WORKLOAD

Distribute a teacher's workload reasonably across the week.

Avoid extremely overloaded days.

====================================================

16. MINIMIZE TEACHER GAPS

Reduce unnecessary free periods between teaching periods.

Preferred:

P1 Teaching
P2 Teaching
P3 Teaching

Less Preferred:

P1 Teaching
P2 Free
P3 Teaching
P4 Free
P5 Teaching

====================================================

17. SPREAD HIGH-FREQUENCY SUBJECTS

Subjects with high weekly counts such as:

- FL
- ENG
- MATH

should be spread throughout the week rather than concentrated on a few days.

====================================================

18. TIMETABLE QUALITY GOAL

Generate the most balanced timetable possible while satisfying all hard constraints.

Hard constraints can never be violated.

Soft constraints should be optimized whenever possible.
